How a cooling tower works

Cooling towers use evaporative cooling: heat is transferred from water to air through evaporation and direct heat exchange. This lowers the temperature of water used in industrial processes or HVAC systems.

  1. Hot water distribution. Warm water is pumped into the tower and spread across the fill media.
  2. Air and water interaction. Fans draw air through the falling water, maximizing contact.
  3. Heat transfer. Evaporative cooling and direct heat exchange pull heat out of the water.
  4. Cooling of water. The water's temperature drops as it descends through the fill.
  5. Release of warm, moist air. Heat and moisture exit through the top of the tower.
  6. Recirculation. The cooled water returns to the process it came from.

Key components: fill material, fans, drift eliminators, nozzles and the cooling basin. Typical applications: power plants, chemical plants, HVAC systems, petroleum refineries and manufacturing facilities.
